A ROSE-COLORED REVIVAL

SAVING THE PINK
PIGEON ON
MAURITIUS

Mauritius’s landscapes shimmer with sapphire lagoons, ivory beaches, volcanic peaks, emerald forests, and offshore islets, each supporting distinct and delicate ecosystems. From coral reefs and coastal wetlands to lowland forests and upland woodlands, the island’s natural richness is remarkable. Yet centuries of human impact — habitat destruction, introduced species, climate change, and extinctions — have dramatically reshaped the island's ecosystems and put its native life at risk.

Despite these challenges, Mauritius offers a powerful story of hope. Intensive conservation work, active habitat restoration, and strong partnerships with local communities are helping to protect the island’s rare and endangered endemic plants and animals. With continued commitment, Mauritius stands poised to reclaim its status as one of the world’s great biodiversity hotspots, showing that even ecosystems once pushed to the brink can be renewed and revived.

A CONSERVATION SUCCESS STORY

By 1991, there were only 10 pink pigeons left in the wild

The pink pigeon is predicted to go extinct in the next 50-100 years without intervention.

The story of evolution is one of constant change, adaptation, and survival against the odds. In the tapestry of avian evolution, the pink pigeon tells a story as complex and vulnerable as that of its dodo relatives.

 Native to Mauritius, this once-thriving bird declined due to human-driven habitat loss, invasive species, and predation—echoing the fate of its extinct neighbors. Yet, thanks to captive breeding and long-term monitoring by the Mauritian Wildlife Foundation and Durrell Wildlife Conservation Trust, its population has rebounded to over 400 individuals.

MAURITIUS
DODO ADVISORY
COMMITTEE

As part of our partnership with Mauritius, Colossal assembled the Mauritius Dodo Advisory Committee, a board of individuals with diverse backgrounds, years of expertise, and direct involvement with the local community. The Committee’s main goal is to provide input on Colossal’s local conservation efforts and aid in the development of a Dodo rewilding program in Mauritius.

The Committee is made up of cultural, commercial, government, and community leaders in Mauritius, who meet quarterly to discuss the feasibility of, concerns over, and needs of the public in regards to the safe and successful reintroduction of the Dodo. Discussions focus on updates from Colossal related to genomics research, biodiversity, environmental restoration, and the sustained wellness of the Mauritian community.
